1. The Situation

Ten settings are operating below their revenue potential. The root cause in most cases is not overstaffing in the conventional sense — it is rooms running below the revenue threshold needed to cover their ratio-mandated staffing floor. You cannot run a baby room with one member of staff because you only have four babies. The two staff are there regardless; the question is how many more babies you need to make that room financially viable.

This means cost reduction through headcount cuts is largely not available at most under-occupied settings — they are already at or near the ratio minimum. The only settings where genuine staffing surplus exists above the ratio floor are Woosehill (reduce by 3 heads, saving £84k) and Northumberland (reduce by 1 head through natural attrition, saving £28k). Every other setting must grow its way out.

The good news: every incremental child placed above the ratio floor threshold generates approximately £4,770 of net EBITDA per year — because the staff cost is already sunk. Filling the 127-child gap across the ten under-occupied settings would deliver over £600k of additional EBITDA at full conversion, or £300k+ at 50% conversion. That, combined with the base case trajectory, takes the group well above £1.3m.

The Ratio Floor Principle

A baby room requires 2 staff whether you have 1 baby or 6 (Ofsted 1:3 ratio). With 4 babies you need 2 staff but generate revenue for only 4 children. Add 2 more babies: same 2 staff, 50% more revenue, zero additional cost. This is why the EBITDA value of each incremental child is so high — the cost is already paid. The recovery plan is therefore primarily an occupancy plan, not a cost-reduction plan.
