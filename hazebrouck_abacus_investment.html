Hazebrouck vs Abacus

Freehold Investment Analysis & Pipeline Comparison — September 2026  |  Alec Hodson, Group CEO  |  CONFIDENTIAL — BOARD USE ONLY

Investment Recommendation

The case for the Hazebrouck freehold is financially viable but not straightforward. At 6.5% over 20 years, annual debt service of £134,203 exceeds the current rent of £55,750 by £78,453/year — this is an asset ownership decision, not a cost-saving one. Hazebrouck's 2026 contribution of £226,420 covers the debt service, leaving headroom of £92,217. The risk is that the setting is currently declining in revenue and that headroom could erode.

Abacus is already rent-free and at full capacity. A freehold investment there adds £134,203/year of debt service with zero rent saving and no room to grow revenue without a capacity expansion. It is not the right use of this capital.

Hazebrouck is the right setting to consider — but the pipeline needs to convert first. Require at least 10 additional confirmed enrolments before exchange of contracts to de-risk the revenue assumption.

4. Why Abacus Is Not the Right Investment Target

Abacus is already rent-free. A £1.5m freehold investment there would add £134,203/year of debt service with zero rent saving — a pure cost increase with no offsetting benefit.

Abacus is also at or near full capacity with 20 enrolled children. Its 72% enquiry loss rate is not a conversion failure — it is a capacity constraint. Growing Abacus revenue requires expanding capacity, which requires planning permission and construction, not a freehold purchase alone.

If the long-term vision is to expand Abacus, the freehold acquisition could be a prerequisite for planning permission. But that is a separate business case requiring planning analysis.

Abacus verdict: Do not invest £1.5m in an Abacus freehold unless accompanied by a clear capacity expansion plan. The debt service of £134,203/year with zero rent offset would reduce group EBITDA by £134,203/year immediately.

The key condition is converting the 14 Contacted enquiries into confirmed places before or at exchange, to demonstrate that occupancy recovery is underway.

6. Recommendation

Proceed with Hazebrouck — subject to four conditions

  1. Occupancy trigger: Require at least 10 additional confirmed enrolments from the current 14 Contacted pipeline before exchange of contracts. This validates the revenue recovery assumption.
  2. Rate floor: Model the deal at 7.0% minimum (£139,554/year debt service). Headroom remains positive at 2026 revenue — confirm this is acceptable before proceeding.
  3. Valuation sense-check: £1.5m for a nursery freehold implies a gross yield of 3.7% on current rent. Benchmark against comparable nursery freehold transactions in the area.
  4. Do not invest in Abacus freehold without a capacity expansion plan. If Abacus expansion is a strategic priority, commission a separate planning and construction feasibility study first.
