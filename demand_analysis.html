Demand Analysis — Abacus vs Hazebrouck

Enquiry pipeline comparison to inform Project Donnington investment decision — September 2026  |  Alec Hodson, Group CEO  |  CONFIDENTIAL

Investment Verdict: Project Donnington belongs in the Abacus catchment

Abacus generates 14.2 enquiries per month — 60% more than Hazebrouck's 8.9. Over 9 months, Abacus attracted 128 enquiries vs Hazebrouck's 80. The "no contact" and "no reason" lost records are not evidence of weak demand — they are evidence of enquiries that arrived faster than the team could respond. The Abacus catchment demand signal is materially stronger.

Hazebrouck's lost reason data is 97% blank — a data quality problem, not a demand problem. But even taking raw enquiry volume alone, Abacus outperforms by 60%. Project Donnington should be in the Abacus catchment. It will fill faster, reach breakeven sooner, and generate stronger long-term contribution.

Hazebrouck data quality flag: 97% of Hazebrouck's lost enquiries have no reason recorded. The Hazebrouck team is not logging lost reasons in Famly. This means we cannot determine whether losses were due to capacity, competition, location or other factors. The raw enquiry volume (8.9/month) is the only reliable demand signal for Hazebrouck. Action: AM to ensure all lost enquiries are logged with a reason going forward.
Abacus "no contact" losses are a process gap, not a demand gap. 20 families enquired at Abacus, were marked as unresponsive, and were lost. These families were in the catchment, wanted childcare, and went elsewhere — likely because they found a place before Abacus could reach them. Project Donnington, with a faster response process in the same catchment, stands to recover a meaningful share of this demand. The 9 Contacted families currently in the Abacus pipeline are the clearest immediate opportunity.

Separate question — what happens to Hazebrouck? The demand analysis answers where to invest in growth. The Hazebrouck decision (close and relocate, buy freehold, or continue) is a separate operational and financial question that should be modelled on its own merits — not driven by demand comparison. The two decisions are independent.
